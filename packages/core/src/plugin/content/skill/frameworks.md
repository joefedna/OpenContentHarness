# Copywriting frameworks

Pick the framework that fits the piece's job. Use it to structure the draft, never as visible labels.

- **AIDA** (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action): ads, landing pages, launch emails.
- **PAS** (Problem, Agitate, Solution): short sales copy and posts about a pain the reader feels now.
- **BAB** (Before, After, Bridge): case studies, transformation posts, testimonials.
- **4 Ps** (Promise, Picture, Proof, Push): offers and sales pages.
- **FAB** (Features, Advantages, Benefits): product pages; always end on the benefit to the reader.
- **Star, Story, Solution**: founder or customer stories that lead into an offer.
- **Hook, Story, Offer**: short-form video and social ads.
- **PASTOR** (Problem, Amplify, Story, Transformation, Offer, Response): long-form sales letters.
- **QUEST** (Qualify, Understand, Educate, Stimulate, Transition): educational emails that convert.
- **SLAP** (Stop, Look, Act, Purchase): very short ads and banners.
- **The 1-3-1 post**: one-line hook, three short supporting lines or points, one-line takeaway or question.
- **Listicle with a spine**: a promise, N items each with a concrete example, and a closing insight that ties them together.
- **Inverted pyramid**: news and announcements, with the most important fact first.
- **Problem-Solution-Proof-CTA**: the default for B2B posts and emails.

## Persuasion devices
- Specific proof beats adjectives: numbers, names, screenshots, quotes, case studies.
- Handle the main objection inside the copy before the reader raises it.
- Use risk reversal (guarantees, free trials) and real scarcity or deadlines only when they are true.
- Social proof: who else uses it, and results that someone like the reader got.
- Write to one reader in second person. Every "we" sentence should earn its place with a benefit to "you".
- One page, one call to action. Make the next step concrete ("Book a 15-minute call", not "Learn more").

## Long-form structure
- Title promising a specific outcome, an intro that states the problem and the payoff within three sentences, scannable headers that say something rather than label something, examples in every section, and a conclusion that tells the reader what to do next.
- For SEO, put the primary keyword in the title, the first 100 words and one header; answer the search intent directly near the top; add an FAQ for related questions; and link to related pages.
