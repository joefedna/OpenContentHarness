# Hooks

The hook is the first line, first second or subject line. Its only job is to earn the next line. A strong hook is specific, aimed at one reader and opens a loop the content closes. Never write a hook the content does not pay off.

## Patterns

| Pattern | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Specific result | I/we did X in Y time | "We cut onboarding from 14 days to 3. Here's the checklist." |
| Contrarian | Common belief is wrong | "Posting every day is why your reach is dying." |
| Mistake | You are probably doing X wrong | "Most founders write their pricing page backwards." |
| Curiosity gap | Hint at a surprising answer | "The best-performing ad we ever ran had no product in it." |
| Number list | N things, with a promise | "7 subject lines that got 60%+ opens." |
| How-to | How to get outcome without pain | "How to hire your first salesperson without wasting a quarter." |
| Call-out | Name the reader | "If you run a 5-person agency, read this before you raise prices." |
| Story open | Drop into a moment | "At 2am the night before launch, our payment provider banned us." |
| Question | A question the reader can't skip | "Would your customers notice if you disappeared tomorrow?" |
| Before/after | Contrast two states | "Last year: 400 followers. Today: 40,000. One change." |
| Bold prediction | The future of X | "In two years, nobody will read your blog. They'll ask an agent." |
| Behind the scenes | Reveal the inside | "Here's the exact doc we send every new client." |
| Stat shock | A surprising real number | "92% of our churned users never finished setup." (only with a real source) |
| Myth vs fact | Name a myth, flip it | "Myth: you need a big list. Fact: our best launch had 312 subscribers." |
| Permission | Relieve guilt | "You don't need a content strategy. You need one good idea a week." |

## Rules
- Open with the most interesting word you have. Cut throat-clearing like "In today's world" or "Let's talk about".
- Use concrete numbers, names, places and timeframes. Round numbers read as made up; real ones read as true.
- One reader, one promise. If a hook speaks to everyone it stops no one.
- Fit the surface: under about 210 characters before the LinkedIn "see more" cut, under 280 for X, the first one to three seconds of a video (say and show it), under about 50 characters for a subject line.
- For video, pair the spoken hook with a visual hook and on-screen text that differ from each other.
- Write many and pick one. Ten hooks in different patterns usually beat one polished hook.
