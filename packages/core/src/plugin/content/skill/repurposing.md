# Repurposing

One strong source can become a week of native content. Repurposing means re-angling for each platform, not shortening.

## Content atomization
1. Start from a pillar: a long article, a podcast or video transcript, a webinar, a case study, a talk, or a long thread.
2. Extract the atoms: the core thesis; three to seven key points; stories and anecdotes; numbers and results; quotable lines; contrarian claims; frameworks or step lists; questions the audience asked.
3. Map each atom to the formats it suits:
   - A story becomes a LinkedIn story post, a short-video script or a newsletter opener.
   - A framework or list becomes a carousel, a thread or an infographic outline.
   - A number or result becomes a stat post, an ad hook or a subject line.
   - A quotable line becomes a quote graphic or a short post.
   - A contrarian claim becomes a hot-take post or a video hook.
   - A question becomes an FAQ, a poll or a reply-bait post.
4. Write each piece with its own hook for that platform's audience, and link pieces back to the pillar.

## Standard sets
- **One long video or podcast:** an SEO article, a newsletter, three to five short clips with scripts and captions, a thread, two LinkedIn posts, a carousel, and quote graphics.
- **One blog article:** a LinkedIn post, a thread, a carousel, a newsletter section, a short video script, and five standalone short posts.
- **One case study:** a before/after post, a story-format short video, an ad with the result as the hook, a sales email, and a landing-page testimonial block.

## Reuse over time
- Re-share proven pieces every 60 to 90 days with a new hook.
- Turn the best comments and replies into new posts.
- Keep a `swipe/` folder of your own winners and analyze why they worked before writing new ones.
